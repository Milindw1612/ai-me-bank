Enterprise AI Roadmap — 0 to 36 Months

How Ai-ME BANK scales from 3 proven, fully-costed flagship pilots (MSME Credit CAM Prep, Fraud Detection, Collections Recovery — see Use Cases) into a bank-wide, governed AI operating model: governance, responsible AI, risk & model validation, audit, data privacy, and security — mapped to RBI and international frameworks, phased across 90 days, 6 months, 12 months, and 3 years.

How to read this page: the 3 use cases already built (MSME Credit CAM Prep, Fraud Detection, Collections Recovery) are the proof points — real architecture, cost, ROI and per-use-case governance already exist for them (see Cost Analysis, Timeline, Governance). This page is the layer above them: the enterprise operating model that lets a bank run dozens of agentic systems safely, not just 3.

Three-Year Vision

From 3 pilots to an enterprise AI Factory

By Month 36, Ai-ME BANK runs agentic AI across every major business line — MSME & retail lending, fraud & AML, collections, treasury, trade finance and customer service — on one governed platform, not a collection of one-off projects.

Every system carries a documented risk tier, an independent model validation record, a PII-safe data path, and a Langfuse-traceable audit trail from day one — not retrofitted after a regulator asks. Governance keeps pace with scale: a 3-lines-of-defense AI risk model, quarterly Board reporting, and an annual Responsible AI transparency report are BAU, not a one-time exercise.

The target external validation point is ISO/IEC 42001 (AI Management System) certification, benchmarked internally against RBI's FREE-AI Framework — proof that the bank's AI program is governed to a standard a regulator, an auditor, and a customer can all independently verify.

Current State

3 pilots governed individually — each has its own risk tier, HITL design and sponsor (see Governance), but there is no bank-wide AI accountability structure above them yet.

→
Enterprise Target State

A standing Board AI & Technology Committee, a CAIO-owned enterprise AI Policy, and a 3-lines-of-defense operating model that scales to dozens of use cases without re-inventing governance each time.

Current State

All 3 pilots already draft-only, human-sign-off design — see Governance → Human-in-the-Loop. No agent holds unilateral authority over money movement, a fraud confirmation, or a lending decision.

→
Enterprise Target State

That same design principle becomes a mandatory enterprise standard — with bias/fairness testing, disclosure, and an Algorithmic Impact Assessment gate applied before any business unit can launch a new agentic system.

Milestones by Horizon
  • 0–3 Mo: Mandate the proven human-in-the-loop pattern (draft/recommend, never unilateral action) as an enterprise standard for any new AI system
  • 3–6 Mo: Publish a bias/fairness testing standard for credit and lending models, building on MSME Credit CAM Prep's disclosed-recommendation design
  • 6–12 Mo: Require an Algorithmic Impact Assessment before any new use case goes live — covering fairness, explainability tier, and human-checkpoint design
  • Year 2: Standardise explainability tiering by risk tier across all business lines (Low/Medium/High risk tiers per Governance)
  • Year 3: Publish an annual enterprise Responsible AI transparency report — the FREE-AI Framework's accountability principle made public and recurring
Grounded in what's already built: Collections Recovery's negotiation is strictly bounded to pre-approved settlement bands, with any deviation or customer pushback escalated to a human officer — this policy-band pattern is the template for every future customer-facing negotiation or outreach agent, not a one-off design.

Milestones by Horizon

  • 3–6 Mo: Independent model validation function live — separate from the build team, reporting to the Model Risk Committee, benchmarked against US SR 11-7 and UK PRA SS1/23 (see Compliance Map → International Benchmarks)
  • 6–12 Mo: Drift monitoring and a kill-switch/rollback standard mandated on all production agents (extends the Langfuse tracing already live per pilot)
  • Year 2: Model risk framework extended to every 2nd-wave use case as it's scoped, not bolted on after launch
  • Year 3: Continuous validation is business-as-usual; an annual model-risk deep-dive goes to the Board AI Committee

  • 3–6 Mo: Formal DPDP compliance certification for the 3 flagship pilots
  • 6–12 Mo: Mandate the tokenise/encrypt/scrub middleware pattern for every new AI system bank-wide, not just the original 3

  • Year 3: Enterprise data-fiduciary maturity audit under the DPDP Act
Current State

Each pilot has a per-release security audit and pen-test scoped into its build (see Cost Analysis and Deploy Guide), but there's no AI-specific threat modelling (prompt injection, model extraction) standard yet.

→
Enterprise Target State

AI-specific threats folded into the bank's existing Cyber Security Framework program, with a recurring AI red-team exercise and a secure LLMOps supply chain.

Milestones by Horizon
  • 0–3 Mo: Build an initial LLM threat model — prompt injection, data exfiltration via agent tool calls, PII leakage in prompts
  • 3–6 Mo: Run the first AI red-team exercise against the 3 live/in-build pilots
  • 6–12 Mo: Fold AI-specific checks into the bank's existing Cyber Security Framework VAPT program, not a parallel process
  • Year 2: Secure LLMOps pipeline enterprise-wide — model/prompt version control, supply-chain checks on any third-party model or agent tool
  • Year 3: Continuous AI red-teaming; CERT-In-aligned incident response runbook specifically for AI systems
