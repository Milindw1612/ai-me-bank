India AI Regulatory Landscape · FY26

RBI, SEBI & National AI Compliance Map

India's banking sector is moving fast on AI — credit, fraud, collections, customer service, liquidity — while regulators move to match it. RBI's FREE-AI Framework (13 Aug 2025), MeitY's India AI Governance Guidelines (5 Nov 2025), SEBI's AI/ML consultation paper, IRDAI's emerging framework, CERT-In's AI-era cybersecurity blueprint, and the DPDP Act now define the dual mandate every large bank faces: accelerate AI-driven innovation while proving board-level accountability for model risk, fairness, explainability and data protection. This page maps every applicable framework directly onto Ai-ME BANK's Enterprise AI Roadmap — sourced from the actual public reports, not paraphrased second-hand.

What it is: on 13 August 2025, the RBI released the Framework for Responsible and Ethical Enablement of AI (FREE-AI) — a committee report chaired by Prof. Pushpak Bhattacharyya (IIT Bombay), giving every RBI-regulated entity a blueprint to "enable AI at scale and control risk with discipline." It is structured as two complementary sub-frameworks: an Innovation Enablement Framework and a Risk Mitigation Framework.
The 7 Sutras (Guiding Principles)
1

Trust is the Foundation

Establishing confidence in AI systems across customers, regulators and staff

2

People First

Prioritising human interests and welfare over pure automation efficiency

3

Innovation over Restraint

Encouraging development while managing risk, rather than defaulting to "no"

4

Fairness and Equity

Ensuring non-discriminatory outcomes across customer segments

5

Accountability

Clear, named responsibility for every AI-driven decision

6

Understandable by Design

Systems built to be transparent and explainable, not explained after the fact

7

Safety, Resilience & Sustainability

Protection against failure, and long-term viability of the AI program itself

The 6 Pillars (26 Recommendations)

Infrastructure

Build on Digital Public Infrastructure, strengthen data quality, prepare model monitoring and fallback paths

Policy

Set adaptive policies that evolve with model capability, use and risk

Capacity

Upskill risk, compliance, audit and frontline teams to work safely and effectively with AI

Governance

Define ownership, decision rights, escalation routes and model documentation standards

Protection

Embed consumer disclosures, consent, recourse and fairness testing into every AI touchpoint

Assurance

Plan independent audits, stress tests and business continuity for AI-dependent processes

Infrastructure, Policy and Capacity form the Innovation Enablement Framework; Governance, Protection and Assurance form the Risk Mitigation Framework.

90-Day Execution Checklist (RBI's own recommended first sprint)
  • Appoint an accountable executive for AI (this is the CAIO mandate on Ai-ME BANK's Roadmap)
  • Inventory all AI use cases (the 3 flagship pilots, 12 agent instances, already documented)
  • Draft a board-approved AI policy
  • Conduct initial fairness testing on customer-facing models
What it is: on 5 November 2025, MeitY unveiled the "India AI Governance Guidelines — Enabling Safe and Trusted AI Innovation" under the IndiaAI Mission, chaired by a drafting committee under Prof. Balaraman Ravindran (IIT Madras). Unlike a new AI law, it proposes a lightweight, adaptive approach built on existing statutes — its core principle is "Do No Harm." The launch was a precursor to the India-AI Impact Summit, 19–20 Feb 2026, New Delhi.
Same 7 Sutras, National Scope

MeitY's guidelines echo the same seven guiding principles as RBI's FREE-AI Framework (Trust, People First, Innovation over Restraint, Fairness & Equity, Accountability, Understandable by Design, Safety/Resilience/Sustainability) — applied across every sector, not just financial services.

6 Pillars of National AI Governance

Data Stewardship

Responsible data sourcing, quality and usage across the AI lifecycle

Risk Mitigation

Identifying and controlling AI-specific risks before they reach production

Regulatory Alignment

Consistency with sector regulators (RBI, SEBI, IRDAI) rather than a parallel AI-only regime

Innovation Enablement

Sandboxes and adaptive rules that don't choke early-stage AI adoption

Public Engagement

Transparency with citizens/customers on how AI affects them

Global Cooperation

Alignment with international norms (EU AI Act, UK FCA, US NIST AI RMF)

The guidelines include an action plan structured across short-, medium- and long-term timelines. For Ai-ME BANK, RBI's FREE-AI Framework remains the primary, sector-specific compliance reference; MeitY's guidelines are treated as the national context RBI's framework itself operates within.
What it is: on 20 June 2025, SEBI issued a consultation paper — "Guidelines for Responsible Usage of AI/ML in Indian Securities Markets" — open for comment until 11 July 2025. It is not yet final; this is a draft-stage framework, tracked for relevance rather than treated as a binding requirement today.
Key Focus Areas
  • Governance — senior management with technical expertise accountable for the full AI model lifecycle
  • Data security and privacy for AI/ML pipelines
  • Bias prevention and mitigation in model outputs
  • Investor protection and disclosure for AI-influenced recommendations
  • Risk controls, with a lighter regime for internal-use models (e.g. surveillance) and stricter controls for client-facing models
  • Full accountability retained by the regulated entity even when AI/ML is outsourced to a vendor
Why This Is Tracked, Not Yet Applied

Ai-ME BANK's 3 flagship pilots (MSME Credit, Fraud, Collections) are RBI-regulated, not SEBI-regulated. SEBI's framework becomes directly relevant only if/when the bank's Wealth & Investment Advisory use case (Wave 3 on the Roadmap) is scoped — at which point the senior-management-accountability and client-facing-model provisions would need a dedicated compliance review before launch.

What it is: IRDAI does not yet have a finalised AI-specific governance framework. On 19 June 2026, it formed a 7-member working group — chaired by Prof. Sandeep Shukla (Director, IIIT Hyderabad) — with a 3-month mandate to recommend the insurance sector's first formal AI governance framework.
Working Group Mandate
  • Recommend an AI governance framework for ethical, transparent, explainable AI in claims and fraud prevention
  • Assess the impact of frontier AI tools on the insurance sector
  • Examine the need for sector-wide AI stress testing
  • Suggest an AI audit framework covering both pre- and post-deployment
  • Identify skill gaps and capacity-building needs across the sector
What Already Applies Today

IRDAI's 2023 Information & Cyber Security Guidelines already mandate a risk-based approach to data security for regulated entities — this is already folded into the AI Security pillar of the Enterprise Roadmap. The forthcoming AI-specific framework is being tracked for any future bancassurance-linked AI use case, not applied retroactively to the 3 current pilots (none of which are insurance-related).

What it is: CERT-In's 2026 cybersecurity blueprint (a 38-page advisory) responds directly to AI-accelerated cyberattacks — threat actors using AI/LLMs to automate vulnerability discovery, exploit development and large-scale attack execution, while AI systems themselves become new attack surfaces (prompt injection, data exfiltration, model theft, training-data poisoning).
Core Mandate
  • Patch critical vulnerabilities on internet-exposed systems within 12 hours of being flagged, where feasible
  • Adopt zero-trust architecture and defence-in-depth as standard design principles
  • Build systems secure-by-design, not secured after the fact
  • Maintain continuous monitoring and visibility into system behaviour
Phase 1 (0–7 Days) — Foundational Governance
  • Multi-factor authentication for all critical access
  • Vulnerability assessment across internet-facing systems
  • Patch known exploited vulnerabilities first
  • Establish incident reporting and baseline monitoring
  • Workforce awareness training on AI-assisted phishing and deepfakes
This phased 0–7-day logic is the direct template for the AI Security pillar's 0–3-month threat-modelling and red-team milestones on the Enterprise Roadmap.
What it is: the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 (with Rules notified in 2025) is India's data protection statute — the legal foundation for every PII control already built into the 3 pilots via shared/middleware.py.
Data Principal Rights
  • Right to access their own personal data
  • Right to correction, completion, updating and erasure
  • Right to nominate someone to exercise these rights on their behalf
  • Right to a grievance-redressal channel
Consent & Data Fiduciary Obligations
  • Consent must be free, specific, informed, unconditional and clear — and as easy to withdraw as it was to give
  • Every consent request must be accompanied by a notice explaining what data, for what purpose, and how to exercise rights or complain
  • Verifiable parental/guardian consent required before processing a child's or a person-with-disability's data
  • No behavioural monitoring or targeted advertising directed at children
  • Penalties for non-compliance of up to ₹250 crore
Every one of these obligations maps directly onto the tokenise (HMAC-SHA256) / encrypt (AES-256-GCM) / scrub (LLM prompt redaction) pattern already proven in code — see Governance → Data Protection & PII.
Why these are here: none of the frameworks below are India-specific or mandatory for Ai-ME BANK — they're included because RBI's FREE-AI Framework and MeitY's guidelines both explicitly call for benchmarking against international standards, and a CAIO candidate is expected to know them.
US Federal Reserve / OCC — SR 11-7 (2011)

"Supervisory Guidance on Model Risk Management" — the foundational, principles-based US standard for managing risk arising from quantitative models in bank decision-making. Its technology-neutral definition of a "model" already covers machine learning and LLM-based systems, even though it predates them.

UK PRA — SS1/23 (2023, in force since 17 May 2024)

The first UK supervisory statement dedicated entirely to model risk management, applying to PRA-regulated firms with internal model approval. Its distinguishing emphasis versus SR 11-7 is explicit board-level accountability for model risk, not just a technical/2nd-line function.

Canada OSFI — E-23

Canada's enterprise-wide model risk management guideline for deposit-taking institutions — the third of the three major global MRM regimes, alongside SR 11-7 and SS1/23, all pre-dating mainstream AI but structurally compatible with it.

Ai-ME BANK's use of this benchmark: the independent Model Risk Committee on the Enterprise Roadmap is explicitly designed against SS1/23's board-accountability emphasis, not just SR 11-7's original technical validation scope — a deliberate choice reflecting where global MRM practice has moved since 2011.
