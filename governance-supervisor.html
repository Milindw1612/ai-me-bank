Concept Design · Program Governance

AI Watchdog Council — an orchestrator over 6 specialist agents

A standing agent system that watches the entire Day 0 → Year 3 initiative the way a strict PMO and compliance officer would — tracking, mapping, flagging and briefing — so the CAIO, Board AI Committee and CEO get one continuously-updated picture instead of quarterly slide decks. It surfaces everything a human needs to decide, in time to decide it. It never decides on its own.

On the word "accountable." This system is deliberately not called accountable. RBI FREE-AI's Sutra 5 defines accountability as clear, named human responsibility for a decision — an entity that cannot decide cannot be accountable. This agent system is responsible for visibility: it watches, maps, tracks and suggests. The CAIO, the Board AI & Technology Committee, and the CEO remain the accountable humans, exactly as defined in AI-GPolicy §6 and the Roadmap's Operating Model RACI.

In Plain English

Think of it as a very attentive chief-of-staff who reads every policy document, every deadline, and every pilot agent's logs every single day, then hands the CAIO a one-page briefing: "these three things need your decision this week; everything else is on track." It never signs anything, hires anyone, or overrides a human — it just makes sure nothing falls through the cracks between now and Year 3.

Each agent has one narrow job, its own data sources, and an explicit list of what it may never do — this separation is what makes each one independently auditable and risk-tierable, rather than one undifferentiated system a Model Risk Committee can't cleanly validate. The Orchestrator's job stays deliberately thin: synthesise the 6 agents' structured findings into one feed, detect cross-domain patterns none of them could see alone, compile the periodic report (see Reporting), and route each item to the correct human by severity (see Escalation & Notification). It performs no independent domain reasoning — giving it that would recreate the "one undifferentiated system" problem the 6-agent split exists to avoid.

How the 6 Agents Coordinate
In Plain English

Think of Regulatory Watch as a scout who reads every new RBI circular and flags "this might matter" — but it's the Compliance Mapping Agent, the actual domain expert, who decides whether it's really a gap in what the bank already does. Everything else flows through the Orchestrator, which either interrupts the CAIO immediately (for anything urgent) or quietly builds up the next scheduled report — and even that report only leaves the building once the CAIO has personally signed it.

Severity-gated, not blanket. High-severity items reach a named human immediately through two channels at once; nothing interrupts anyone for a Low-severity item.

Notification Channels
  • ✓ High severity → CAIO: high-importance email + the bank's existing enterprise messaging tool (e.g. Teams), sent together, plus logged to the dashboard simultaneously — carrying the finding and the source agent's suggested options inline, not just "check the dashboard"
  • ✓ High severity → Board: formal high-importance email routed through the Company Secretary's office / the bank's existing board-portal software — never a direct ping to an individual director
  • ✕ No WhatsApp or consumer messaging apps — official bank business stays inside governed, auditable, retainable enterprise channels; using a personal app for compliance or fraud-adjacent alerts would contradict the DPDP/PII rigor built into the rest of this program
  • ✓ Escalation ladder: an unacknowledged High-severity item escalates to a phone call after a defined window (e.g. 2–4 hours) as the last resort
  • ✓ Medium/Low severity: dashboard only — surfaced at the CAIO's normal review cadence, no interruption, to keep the High-severity channel credible and not desensitised by over-alerting
The report is drafted, never sent, by the system. The Orchestrator compiles it — every agent contributing its own section — but it always stops at "Draft Report." Nothing reaches the Board, the Audit Committee, or RBI until the CAIO has personally reviewed and signed it. Same principle as every other output on this page: this system surfaces, a named human decides.
Report Structure — Compiled by the Orchestrator, One Section per Agent
  1. Executive Summary — the Orchestrator's own synthesis: overall program health, headline items
  2. Compliance Mapping status — gaps opened and closed this period
  3. Program Progress — checklist and milestone status, delays, budget
  4. Agent Fleet Status — Validation Gate results per pilot, drift or violations this period
  5. Audit & Assurance — open and closed non-conformities, process-compliance status
  6. Regulatory Watch highlights — new or amended rules detected this period, and their relevance
  7. New Initiatives — anything the Intake & Integration Agent processed this period, if applicable
  8. Sign-Off Block — reviewed and approved by the named Chief AI Officer, dated

Who Builds It, and When

Built by the same core team already defined in cost.html's Team & Resource Rate Cards — AI/ML Engineers, Backend Engineers, DevOps — not a new hiring line. It cannot start at Day 0: there is nothing to supervise until the checklist, the policy, and at least one live pilot exist. Recommended sequencing: scope and start the build at the 3–6 Month horizon (once MSME Credit CAM Prep is live and generating real data), reaching full operation across all 4 continuous agents by the 6–12 Month horizon, once all 3 flagship pilots are live and there's a complete data surface to monitor.

Quality Control on the Watchdog Council's Own Output
  • ✓ Pre-launch: runs its own shadow-mode validation period, its flags compared against what a human PMO/compliance officer actually caught, must clear a Validation Gate before being trusted operationally — same discipline as Fraud Detection's shadow-mode
  • ✓ Post-launch: the Model Risk Committee — the existing 2nd-line function in the Roadmap's Operating Model RACI — periodically reviews its flagging accuracy
  • ✓ Internal Audit folds it into the AI-audit-universe scoping already planned for 6–12 Months
Risk Classification & Access

Classified as Tier 0 under the existing Risk Classification Framework (Section 7) — the highest tier, given its read access across every pilot's audit trail and every governance artifact — even though its Autonomy Level is A0–A1 (observation and recommendation only, per Section 8).

  • ✓ Named human owner: the Chief AI Officer, who reviews its outputs — not the agent itself
  • ✓ Every finding and every routing decision is logged to the audit trail — an auditor can ask "why did this get flagged Medium and not High"
  • ✓ Read-only access everywhere — no write access to production systems, HR systems, or the pilot agents it monitors
